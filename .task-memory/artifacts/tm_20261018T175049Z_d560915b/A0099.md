V2-043I planning round, batch 1 (Codex-luna, Codex-sol, DeepSeek). Three plans, converged on the same core model.
SHARED DECISIONS
D1 - The nesting is a presentation projection computed from the rows the controller already holds (controller.sessions). No new request, no per-parent read, no controller state, no new network call. The catalog's flat order stays available in the model (groupSessionsByProject) and the nesting is a second projection of it.
D2 - The depth is recursive: a grandchild nests under the child the endpoint named as its parent, because the recorded evidence (test/contract/fixtures/opencode/2.0.22/d/foreground-nested.json) has a real three-level lineage and a one-level rule would hoist a grandchild wrongly. Only the visual indent is capped (DeepSeek: 3 levels / 36 px plus a guide) so a phone keeps the content width; deeper rows still nest and order correctly.
D3 - The order: the roots keep the server's order among the roots and the siblings keep the server's order among the siblings; inside a branch the parent is rendered first and its children follow immediately. The branch occupies the position of the earliest of its rows in the server's order (Codex-luna) or the parent's own position among the roots (Codex-sol); the server's order cannot stay literally identical when a child precedes its parent. No client sort key (title or updatedAt) is ever introduced.
D4 - The orphan (the parent is not in the same group): the child stays a root of its own group, in the server's position, and is marked as a child whose parent is not shown; it is never dropped, never moved to the end and never given an invented parent or a synthetic group. Its own available children still nest under it.
D5 - The cross-project case: the project attribution wins; a child reported in another group (or with no project) is never moved into the parent's group; two rows with no project share the unattributed group and may form a tree.
D6 - The live behaviour: the items are recomputed from the published rows on every build; the quiet re-read replaces them atomically. A child that arrives before its parent renders as a marked root in that frame and nests in the next read; nothing is cached and nothing is collapsed, so no row flickers out or disappears.
D7 - The affordances: the nested row reuses the existing row untouched (the actions menu with the rename, delete with the cascade warning, fork, mark viewed and hide, the ownership label, the unread mark and the tap-to-expand). No collapse toggle and no 'return to parent' (the parent is the adjacent row above). The additions are display-only: the indentation, a vertical guide, a 'child session' label and the orphan label. A fork lineage never nests: fork provenance is distinct from task delegation.
D8 - The invariant: every session of the visible list produces exactly one row with the same SessionSummary; no session, project or parent link is invented, and a cycle emits the remaining row at depth 0 (DeepSeek's visited-set guard).
DIVERGENCES
V1 - The branch's position: Codex-luna places the branch at the earliest of its rows in the server's order; Codex-sol keeps the parent at its own position among the roots and does not promote it to the child's earlier position; DeepSeek keeps the roots in the server's order and moves only the children below their parent. The orchestrator must pick one and state the rule.
V2 - The depth cap: only DeepSeek proposes a capped visual indent (3 levels, 36 px plus a guide); the others leave the depth uncapped.
V3 - The labels: all three propose a visible marker (a 'parent not shown' label and a 'child session' label) and DeepSeek places it on the ownership line; the exact copy and its locale are not settled.
