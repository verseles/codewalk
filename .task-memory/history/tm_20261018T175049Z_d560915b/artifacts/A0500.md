## Complete Findings
Dart Timer.new invokes a callback after its duration and delegates timer creation to the current Zone; the page does not promise that an async test resumes at an exact wall-clock instant. [Timer.new](https://api.dart.dev/dart-async/Timer/Timer.html). Timer.cancel prevents the timer callback after cancellation and is safe to repeat. This alone does not prove whether a particular local cancellation ran before a callback. [Timer.cancel](https://api.dart.dev/dart-async/Timer/cancel.html).
The Dart-maintained fake_async1.3.3 package explicitly supports deterministic Futures, Streams, Timers and microtasks: tests advance a controlled clock and fire scheduled events without waiting for real time. Its sample uses fakeAsync and elapse. It cannot control DateTime.now or Stopwatch directly; clock-package alternatives integrate with it. [fake_async](https://pub.dev/packages/fake_async).
Inference: a test that relies on short real waits may be a candidate for controlled-time reproduction, but documentation does not establish the cause of any local failure or authorize changing product behavior, increasing waits or suppressing checks. Inspect local scheduling and reproduce first.
